About

Sam Mozaffari

I am an Experience Designer in Sydney. I have spent seven years in design, physical products first and then digital ones, and for the last two I have designed how operational services work for a network of 900 restaurants and a billion-dollar business.

I work at the point where research turns into a decision. I design studies that can prove the business wrong, turn what they find into models the operators recognise as their own, and run the conversations where priorities get set and owned. The changes that came out of that work have shipped to a workforce of more than 50,000 people.

Most of my research pipeline is AI-assisted now. I design the extraction, the typed records it produces and the human gate every record passes through, and I build the same discipline into the tools I make for other people.

How I work

From the decision backwards

Start from the decision

Before any research starts I want to know which decision it has to inform and who is going to make it. The methods follow from that, not from a list of what a designer is supposed to produce.

Evidence with provenance

Every insight can be traced back to the interview, the site visit, the survey row or the ticket it came from. If it has no source it does not go in the register, and if it is not in the register it does not go on the roadmap.

Design the system, not the screen

Operational services break in the handoffs: between a person and a system, a rule and a habit, one team and the next. I map every lane and design where they meet, and the fix is sometimes a feature, sometimes a routine and sometimes a rule.

Agents draft, people decide

I build the harness around the model: the roles, the tools, the typed outputs and the gates between them. Nothing generated becomes a plan until a person has looked at it, and that gate is a step in the process rather than a promise.

A book and some tooling

On the side

I wrote The Agentic Service Designer to work out, in detail, what service design methods look like when agents do the first draft and humans hold the gates. The open tooling behind it is in the Library. Both are personal projects built with AI assistance, and the worked example runs on a ferry operator written for the book.
